General overview

A course with an emphasis on the interpretation of research results. It presents the basic precepts and underlying principles of the primary methods of epidemiological data analysis and statistical methods.

  • A general introduction to the basic statistical concepts used in biomedical and public health applications
  • Main goal: a conceptual understanding of the principles of scientific thinking and of epidemiological data analysis, with emphasis on interpreting the current scientific literature

The path through the course (1/2)

  1. Science before statistics: description, causality and prediction as aims of data analysis; research designs and research questions
  2. Describing data: tables, graphs, distributions and summary statistics as reported in medical journals; measures of disease occurrence, association and risk

The path through the course (2/2)

  1. Statistical inference: hypothesis testing, estimation, and the analysis of continuous and categorical data
  2. Stratified analysis and multivariable models: linear, multiple and logistic regression, and the Cox proportional hazards model — with emphasis on how they are reported and interpreted in the literature
  3. Time-to-event data: survival analysis closes the course

Principal aims (1/2)

  • Introduce basic epidemiological and methodological concepts of health research, up to the interpretation of the most common statistical analyses in the biomedical literature
  • Acquire the ability to explain and demonstrate the advantages of adequately describing data as a first step of the scientific method

Principal aims (2/2)

  • Develop skills in the description, presentation, interpretation and summary of data
  • Objectively analyze and interpret the quality and reporting of the analyses and results of biomedical scientific research

Who is this course for?

  • Anyone interested in the principles of epidemiological data analysis, from any biomedical or related profession
  • Participants are recommended to have completed a basic course in epidemiological study design
  • The main focus is on interpretation, rather than on direct analytical skills
