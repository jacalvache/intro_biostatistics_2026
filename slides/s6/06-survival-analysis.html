Longitudinal studies

  • During the follow-up of research subjects, several problems can occur that make their characterization and description difficult.
  • Loss to follow-up
    • Dropping out of the study
    • Difficulties contacting the subjects under observation
    • Occurrence of multiple events

Sometimes, the calculation of the cumulative incidence is insufficient to describe more complex situations appropriately.

Time to …

Time until the event of interest or outcome occurs

Main feature of survival analysis: censoring

M = lost to follow-up; * = end of study without having had the outcome

Survival analysis?

  • It is a group of statistical techniques in which the times until the occurrence of an event are analyzed, rather than the simple occurrence of the event.
  • Appropriate for analyzing longitudinal time-to-event data in the presence of censoring.
  • In these situations other statistical methods are insufficient or provide invalid results.

Summarizing survival data

  • T = survival time (or time to an event)
  • Usually right-skewed distributions
  • Data are summarized using,
    • Life tables
    • Survival curves calculated with the Kaplan-Meier method
    • Median survival and quartiles

Strategies for analyzing survival data

  • Estimate a function that gives the probability of the outcome as a function of time
  • Identify risk factors for a better or worse survival prognosis
  • Compare the survival of different groups

Survival curve

  • T = time to the event (T > 0)

\[S(t) = P(T > t), \quad t \geq 0\]

  • Proportion of subjects “alive” after time t
  • Decreasing function
